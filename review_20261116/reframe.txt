This uniform framewise schedule creates sustained GPU load across dialogue states whose computation has strong, reusable temporal structure. ReFrame formulates full-duplex inference as a silence-aware computation problem. Mutual Silence Speculation ( MSS ) operates when both participants are silent: it evaluates multi-frame Temporal continuations, verifies text and hidden-state stability, and applies batched Mimi audio-token correction. Listening Frame Folding ( LFF ) operates while the user speaks and the agent listens: it processes already-arrived user frames through parallel Temporal execution and frame-batched audio-token generation while accepting only the verified prefix. Frame-Budget Reinvestment ( FBR ) assigns the released frame intervals to resumable external retrieval and language-model workers, then injects timely references through Moshi’s conditioning interface. We instantiate ReFrame on five full-duplex models spanning Moshi-family, phase-structured, and flattened autoregressive generation. Across the complete 1,225-conversation FDBench, ReFrame reduces measured GPU execution time by 18.80–31.01% and retains 96.92–100% non-PAD text agreement on five different models. On HaluEval tests, FBR increases the number of correct generated spoken answers from 86 to 205 under the same auxiliary-service budget. ReFrame turns recurring dialogue structure into lower inference cost and higher end-to-end service capability. 1 Introduction Figure 1: ReFrame routes mutual silence to MSS and agent-listening intervals to LFF, while retaining dense execution during agent speech. The frame budget released by MSS and LFF is reinvested through FBR. Spoken dialogue between users and intelligent agents is an important human-computer interaction paradigm. Full-duplex models that simultaneously generate responses and receive user input have attracted growing attention because they support real-time barge-in and lightweight backchannels, offering a conversational experience closer to human interaction ( Défossez et al., 2024 ; Fang et al., 2026 ; Roy et al., 2026 ) . This capability, however, imposes strict per-frame latency constraints on model inference. The system must produce one audio frame within a small time budget. For example, Moshi emits one Mimi frame every 80 ms at 12.5 Hz. Meeting this fixed frame rate with dense execution requires the complete inference process at every frame and leaves little room for deeper reasoning or knowledge access during the interaction. A full-duplex model operating on speech as its primary modality also spends a large fraction of every conversation in silence. In routine full-duplex services, the agent remains silent for up to 84% of interaction frames. Existing systems have largely focused on using these silent intervals for additional internal reasoning, such as chain-of-thought-style latent computation, to improve response quality ( Wu et al., 2026 ; Huang et al., 2026 ) . These approaches improve model capability through additional computation, but they also increase inference cost and make per-frame latency constraints more challenging to satisfy. The characteristic output regularity of silent intervals provides a complementary opportunity to reduce foreground model computation. Moreover, prior work has mostly focused on the agent’s “listening” phase, in which the user speaks while the agent remains silent. The complementary regime of mutual silence, where neither party speaks, has received far less attention. We observe that these two silence regimes have different temporal structure and therefore expose different opportunities for reducing inference cost. ReFrame converts both regimes into complementary, low-cost inference paths. Figure 1 summarizes its state-aware execution and compute-reinvestment workflow. For mutual silence, Mutual Silence Speculation ( MSS ) extends the recent silent state across a block of frames and verifies the continuation with a multi-position Temporal pass. For the listening regime, Listening Frame Folding ( LFF ) buffers already-arrived user frames and evaluates their Temporal positions and within-frame Depth Transformer steps together while preserving autoregression across codebook levels. Both mechanisms reduce dense per-frame model computation with almost no loss of output fidelity. On the full FDBench set of 1,225 full-duplex spoken interactions, ReFrame reduces Joint GPU cost by 18.80–31.01% across all five major full-duplex speech models while retaining 99.33–100% text agreement. Across the three Moshi checkpoints, non-PAD text agreement is 96.92–98.12%, while BayLing-Duplex and PersonaPlex preserve exact trajectories across all 1,225 conversations. Frame-Budget Reinvestment ( FBR ) uses the compute reclaimed by MSS and LFF for resumable auxiliary tasks and injects timely references into ongoing full-duplex responses. Across five spoken knowledge and reasoning evaluations, FBR delivers more timely, correct references. A HaluEval evaluation further shows an increase in correct generated spoken answers from 86 to 205. We introduce ReFrame and make the following contributions. • We develop MSS, which reuses verified state during mutual silence, and LFF, which folds already-arrived user frames during the agent’s listening state into a parallel multi-position block. • We adapt and optimize these mechanisms across multiple full-duplex models, covering Moshi and Moshi-based multi-stream architectures with hierarchical audio-token generation and the multi-channel interleaved BayLing-Duplex sequence. • We introduce FBR, which converts the saved GPU budget into timely external retrieval and resident language-model computation, and injects the resulting references into ongoing full-duplex responses. 2 Related Work Full-duplex speech models and silence structure. Moshi introduced a real-time speech-text architecture with hierarchical Mimi audio-token generation, a Temporal Transformer, and an autoregressive Depth Transformer (Depformer). PersonaPlex builds controllable conversational roles and voices on a related full-duplex architecture. BayLing-Duplex instead represents interleaved speech streams with a single autoregressive language model. Synchronous LLMs incorporate explicit time information into language-model generation to align full-duplex responses with real-world interaction ( Veluri et al., 2024 ) , while OmniFlatten unifies speech and text in a flattened sequence for low-latency full-duplex generation ( Zhang et al., 2025 ) . LSLM fuses listening and speaking channels for real-time turn-taking ( Ma et al., 2024 ) , SoulX-Duplug offloads dialogue management to a lightweight module ( Yan et al., 2026 ) , and VoxMind extends end-to-end spoken dialogue toward agentic interaction ( Liang et al., 2026 ) . Recent studies further examine user-stream routing while speaking, synchronization, and turn-taking behavior ( Lu et al., 2026 ; Riera et al., 2026 ) . Turn-taking research also establishes prosody and voice-activity projection as useful signals for anticipating conversational transitions ( Ekstedt and Skantze, 2022 ; Inoue et al., 2024 ) . Full-Duplex-Bench and its overlap-focused extension provide complementary evaluations of turn-taking and simultaneous speech ( Lin et al., 2025 ; Lin et al., 2026 ) . These models deliver highly interactive speech generation under a strict per-frame latency budget, and their inputs arrive continuously so that inference remains active at every audio frame. A large fraction of this interaction is spent in silence, but existing systems treat mutual silence and listening identically. Speculative and parallel decoding. Speculative decoding uses a smaller model to propose multiple candidate tokens and the full model to verify them. Its key idea is to replace multiple sequential decoding steps with one parallel verification pass, reducing the target model overhead when several consecutive candidates are accepted ( Leviathan et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2023 ) . Follow-up methods predict several future positions with additional decoding heads, as in Medusa ( Cai et al., 2024 ) , or use feature-level uncertainty and adaptive candidate trees, as in EAGLE and EAGLE-2 ( Li et al., 2025b ; Li et al., 2024 ) . These methods target autoregressive streams in which future output positions can be proposed and verified against a fixed conditioning context. Extending this paradigm to full-duplex speech introduces several constraints. Future user frames are exogenous inputs and are unavailable before arrival. Speech generation also couples semantic outputs with multiple dependent codec-token streams, which makes exact agreement over a multi-frame proposal difficult to maintain. A mismatch can affect both cross-frame Temporal states and within-frame codec-token dependencies and may require state restoration and suffix replay under a strict frame deadline. ReFrame exploits the state-specific temporal regularities of full-duplex speech models. Auxiliary computation in spoken dialogue. MoshiRAG studies asynchronous knowledge augmentation for full-duplex speech models by invoking an auxiliary language model to improve factual responses ( Chien et al., 2026 ) . DuplexOmni adds listening, visual perception, thinking, and speaking capabilities to real-time multimodal interaction. The Silent Thought models latent internal reasoning for full-duplex spoken dialogue to improve response quality. Retrieval-augmented language models connect generation to external corpora through learned retrieval, including REALM and RETRO ( Guu et al., 2020 ; Borgeaud et al., 2022 ) . Dense Passage Retrieval, ColBERT, and unsupervised dense retrieval develop effective candidate-search and ranking mechanisms ( Karpukhin et al., 2020 ; Khattab and Zaharia, 2020 ; Gao et al., 2022 ) , while Fusion-in-Decoder integrates retrieved passages into generation ( Izacard and Grave, 2021 ) . Adaptive-RAG, FLARE, and IRCoT schedule retrieval according to question complexity, generation uncertainty, or intermediate reasoning ( Jeong et al., 2024 ; Jiang et al., 2023 ; Trivedi et al., 2023 ) . Systems such as FlexGen, Splitwise, and SGLang improve language-model serving through memory-aware execution, phase specialization, and structured runtime support ( Sheng et al., 2023 ; Patel et al., 2024 ; Zheng et al., 2024 ) . These efforts focus on knowledge access, multimodal capability, or response quality, and they typically increase rather than reduce inference cost. They do not address how reclaimed foreground compute can be safely reused within the per-frame latency budget. 3 Methods 3.1 Preliminaries A full-duplex spoken dialogue model receives a user audio frame x t x_{t} and generates an agent output frame y t y_{t} at each step of a fixed frame rate. FBR assigns the resulting GPU intervals to auxiliary computation within the frame budget. 3.3 Mutual Silence Speculation (MSS) During sustained mutual silence, when both the user and agent remain silent for several frames, full-duplex models repeatedly emit a silent text token and produce highly similar Temporal hidden states across neighboring frames. Their Mimi audio tokens retain small frame-level variations, while the decoded signal remains perceptually silent and preserves the same interaction state. This combination yields a predictable silent-text continuation, a relatively stable cross-frame representation, and benign audio-token variation. MSS turns this regularity into blockwise silent-state speculation: it proposes several upcoming silent transitions together, evaluates their Temporal positions in one multi-position block, and retains the verified prefix. Speculation and staged verification. For each mutual-silence interval, MSS activates after both streams have remained silent for several frames, the Temporal hidden states exhibit high similarity, and user energy remains low. It repeats the latest verified silent user input and silent text token to propose K R K_{R} candidate frames, including their text predictions, Temporal hidden states, and agent-side Mimi audio tokens. As mutual silence deepens, the K R K_{R} window grows dynamically to capture longer speculative spans and improve blockwise execution efficiency. The K R K_{R} candidate positions are evaluated in one Temporal block. Verification scans the resulting text predictions and hidden states in temporal order. A candidate joins the accepted prefix when its text remains in the silent continuation and the cosine similarity between its hidden state and the verified silence anchor exceeds the threshold. If position j j fails, positions 1 , … , j − 1 1,\ldots,j-1 form the accepted prefix and the remaining suffix returns to dense framewise execution. After Temporal acceptance, one batched Depformer pass evaluates all accepted positions and replaces the proposed token values from the first N v N_{v} codebook levels with their computed values; for Moshi, N v = 3 N_{v}=3 corresponds to CB0–CB2. These corrected token values become feedback for subsequent frame computation. Depformer KV is local to codebook generation within one frame and is reset across frames. Cross-frame history resides in the Temporal KV cache. Correction retains the accepted speculative Temporal hidden states and KV entries, without sequentially replaying them under the corrected tokens. The higher residual codebook levels describe fine variations that remain perceptually silent in this state, so they retain the proposed silence values. Staged verification therefore combines exact silent text predictions, stable verified hidden states, and batched partial audio-token correction. Each accepted frame waits in the candidate buffer. At every real user-frame arrival, the router confirms that the observed input matches the silent continuation and releases the corresponding cached agent frame. When user speech begins, MSS commits the already verified prefix, discards the remaining silent candidates, and resumes dense framewise execution from the resulting state. The same principle maps naturally to other generation structures. A multi-channel interleaved model proposes a silent token block, verifies the prefix exactly, and retains the verified positions. PersonaPlex uses its stable period-two silence pattern to propose an alternating two-frame block, then verifies the text token and all eight agent-side Mimi audio tokens at every accepted position. Across architectures, MSS converts a persistent mutual-silence state into verified block execution. 3.4 Listening Frame Folding (LFF) When the user speaks while the agent listens, each frame contributes new user audio. This continuously changing user input makes last-frame silence reuse insufficient for prediction. The agent, however, commonly maintains a silent listening state across several consecutive frames, creating an opportunity to fold their computation. LFF groups K L K_{L} already-arrived user frames, evaluates their cross-frame states in one masked multi-position Temporal operation, generates candidate audio with one frame-batched Depformer operation, and then validates the agent-side feedback. The router enters LFF after detecting sustained user speech together with agent silence, and returns to framewise execution when the agent begins a non-silent response or the listening interval ends. For multi-channel interleaved models such as BayLing-Duplex, LFF folds packed per-frame token groups into one masked block and repairs the rejected suffix in the unified autoregressive cache. 3.5 Model-State Repair and Buffered Frame Output ReFrame distinguishes the frame released as model output from the frame stored as feedback for subsequent inference. Full-duplex models produce output at a fixed frame rate; Moshi, for example, emits one frame every 80 ms at 12.5 Hz. A persistent output queue preserves this frame rate, while the verified frame sequence supplies the text, audio tokens, and Temporal state used by later computation. MSS caches its verified silent continuation and associates each cached position with the corresponding subsequently arriving user frame. When that user frame confirms continued silence, the cached agent frame becomes both the released output and the frame committed to the autoregressive model state. At user-speech onset, MSS commits the verified prefix, discards the remaining candidates, and restores the Temporal cache at the prefix endpoint. MSS therefore advances output and autoregressive state with the same verified frame sequence. LFF uses this separation during its one-time queue initialization. The K L − 1 K_{L}-1 added silent frames enter only the output queue and never become part of the autoregressive context or Temporal feedback. Frames accepted by each folded Temporal operation are committed as model feedback and appended to the queue; a recomputed suffix follows the same path after repair. The initialization therefore maintains silent output at the required frame rate without altering the state that conditions subsequent dialogue. 3.6 Frame-Budget Reinvestment (FBR) MSS and LFF shorten per-frame model execution while output frames continue at the model’s fixed frame rate. Our model suite contains Moshiko and Moshika, Moshika-RL ( Ohashi et al., 2026 ) , BayLing-Duplex, and PersonaPlex, covering multiple full-duplex architectures, generation policies, and model scales. We evaluate FBR on five spoken knowledge and reasoning workloads. The RAG-retrieval evaluation uses HaluEval ( Li et al., 2023 ) , OpenAudioBench ( Li et al., 2025a ) , and WebQuestions ( Berant et al., 2013 ) , whereas the LLM-query evaluation uses OpenAudioBench, SVAMP ( Patel et al., 2021 ) , and ASDiv ( Miao et al., 2021 ) . These tasks span factual knowledge, audio-related facts, open-domain questions, and arithmetic reasoning. Comparison methods. Dense executes the complete forward and generation process at every frame. MSS enables mutual-silence speculation alone, LFF enables listening frame folding alone, and Joint routes between both mechanisms according to the observed dialogue state. We compare all three methods against Dense in both efficiency and quality. For FBR, Dense and Joint run the same retrieval worker on the same questions with the same waiting configuration and service allocation. We compare actual reference injections and correct spoken answers. Metrics. GPU cost reduction is the relative decrease in CUDA execution time measured over the complete interaction. Share is the fraction of all interaction frames in the state exploited by each mechanism: sustained mutual silence for MSS, user speech with a silent agent for LFF, and their union for Joint. The committed span n c n_{c} is the mean number of frame positions that each optimized multi-frame execution verifies and commits; it measures the useful work obtained from one speculative or folded execution. For BayLing-Duplex, each serialized 15-token output block represents five logical frame positions, and n c n_{c} is reported in these frame units. Quality metrics use Dense as the reference. Text is the exact agreement of agent text tokens at corresponding frame positions. Codebook Δ \Delta is the fraction of all interaction frames in which Dense emits speech and the optimized complete output-token frame differs from Dense. WER Δ \Delta is the normalized word edit distance between Whisper transcripts of the complete Dense and optimized agent waveforms under the same transcription and text normalization pipeline. Onset@3 is the fraction of Dense text-onset anchors whose matched optimized onset is emitted within three frame periods. Preserving auxiliary progress across frame intervals lets retrieval and LLM workers use the reclaimed time to strengthen ongoing full-duplex responses. 5 Conclusion ReFrame establishes dialogue state as a direct source of systems efficiency for full-duplex spoken interaction. Mutual Silence Speculation (MSS) converts persistent silence into verified Temporal blocks with batched Mimi audio-token correction, while Listening Frame Folding (LFF) converts already-arrived user frames during listening into parallel model execution. Architecture-aware state verification and repair adapt these mechanisms to Moshi and Moshi-based models with distinct silence patterns, as well as multi-channel interleaved BayLing-Duplex. On the full FDBench, their composition consistently reduces measured GPU cost across the evaluated models while preserving near-identical text and high speech fidelity. Frame-Budget Reinvestment (FBR) turns the released frame intervals into auxiliary computation, enabling the model to provide more correct answers in a timely manner and thereby improve service quality. These results connect lower frame-level inference cost to higher end-to-end service capability, making state-aware computation a practical foundation for efficient and knowledge-augmented full-duplex agents. Appendix D HaluEval FBR Protocol and Additional Analysis The HaluEval evaluation continues generation after conditioning on admitted references and scores the generated speech. Speech inference and retrieval share one A100. The native reference encoder runs as the same external A100 service for Dense and Joint. Its latency is included in the admission deadline. Thus, the experiment matches the complete service allocation across methods. The conditioner consumes reference embeddings in order, with its consumption offset restored on rollback. Reference completion alone is not an injection: the reference must be encoded, admitted, and consumed by subsequent Temporal forwards. Retrieval, reference encoding, and injection use the available frame budget, with one extra frame allowed at the model’s retrieval trigger. Scoring. Whisper large-v3-turbo transcribes the actual generated audio. A local Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct judge uses the retained dataset-specific answer rubric, greedy decoding, and identical prompts without method labels or timing information. The score measures the correctness of the required answer, not factual correctness of every additional sentence. Dedicated evidence scoring separately checks consumed references. Normalized answer-alias matching is retained as a lexical diagnostic and increases from 73 to 175 on HaluEval.
